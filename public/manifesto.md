> Official Charter, Constitution, and Articles of Governance of the Hmar Heritage Foundation.

---

## Article I · Name, Legal Status & Statutory Mandate
- **Section 1.1 Name & Organization:** The organization is established as the **Hmar Heritage Foundation**, operating as an independent, non-political non-profit dedicated to language and digital preservation.
- **Section 1.2 Origins & Mandate:** Started in 2024 as a digital preservation initiative and formally constituted in 2026, the Foundation provides contributor tools, open schemas, and project datasets. Its focus is building public digital standards for future generations.
- **Section 1.3 Public Benefit Mandate:** The Foundation is established strictly for community welfare, language preservation, education, and cultural upliftment.

---

## Article II · Statutory Project Units & Digital Infrastructure
- **Section 2.1 Hmar Digital Library:** Outlines the planned 1-stop public download storefront for classic literature, rare manuscripts, folk songbooks, and compiled software locale bundles.
- **Section 2.2 Open Locale Project:** Establishes language-agnostic software internationalization schemas, Supabase staging workflows, and multi-platform i18n translation key formats.
- **Section 2.3 Hmar Open Lexicon:** Develops open Hmar-English and Hmar-Mizo lexical databases, definitions, etymology, and open lookup APIs across dual web applications.
- **Section 2.4 Hmar Wikipedia Incubator Initiative:** Organizes institutional stewardship, article drafting (10 lead-section articles weekly), and encyclopedic development on Wikimedia Incubator Wp/hmr.
- **Section 2.5 Hmar Heritage Archival Project:** Operates the backend data ingestion engine, low-friction anonymous upload interface, GitHub staging pipeline, and Hugging Face dataset hub mirroring.

---

## Article III · Linguistic Autonym Sovereignty & Cultural Identity
- **Section 3.1 Language Classification:** The Foundation recognizes Hmar as an independent language within the **Zo** language family.
- **Section 3.2 Academic Exonyms:** Terms such as Kuki-Chin, Chin-Kuki, or Kuki-Chin-Naga are external academic labels used by researchers to classify the Zo language family.
- **Section 3.3 Metadata Rights & Self-Determination:** The Foundation asserts sole community authority over orthography, glossaries, and digital metadata representation across global software registries, ISO 639-3 standards, and Unicode CLDR.

---

## Article IV · Open Stewardship & Anti-Paywall Protection
- **Section 4.1 Permitted Commercial Integration:** All Foundation software translation keys, i18n glossaries, and dataset repositories remain 100% free for open-source maintainers and commercial software developers to build upon and integrate into applications.
- **Section 4.2 Anti-Exploitation Clause:** Selling, paywalling, or commercializing the raw PDF archival scans, dictionary databases, or raw datasets as standalone proprietary products is strictly prohibited.

---

## Article V · Public Stewardship & Financial Integrity
- **Section 5.1 Public Trust Governance:** All assets, archives, software repositories, and domain infrastructure are maintained strictly in public trust for the benefit of the community and future generations.
- **Section 5.2 Financial Auditing & Non-Profit Integrity:** The society adheres to transparent financial auditing, non-profit fund allocation, and public accountability standards.

---

## Article VI · Generational Scope & Unattributed Foundation Work
- **Section 6.1 Generational Timeline:** The society acknowledges that language preservation and digital infrastructure are open, continuous, and generational endeavors. The Foundation does not condition its operations upon immediate commercial adoption or short-term results.
- **Section 6.2 Open Contribution:** The Foundation focuses on quiet, practical groundwork. All open datasets, locale keys, and digital archives are released under open licenses so third parties can build upon them without requiring personal attribution to individual contributors or the society.
